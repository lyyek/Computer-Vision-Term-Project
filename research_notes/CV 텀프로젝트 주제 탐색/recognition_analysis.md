## Q1. Seed 1 — Training-free OVSS with CLIP/DINO: state of the art, saturation, and is a "why does it work" question still open?

### Takeaway
Training-free OVSS is **very saturated as a method race**. On the standard 8-benchmark average (ViT-B/16), scores went from about 38 (SCLIP/ClearCLIP, ECCV'24) to 44.4 (CASS, CVPR'25), 48.6 (Trident, ICCV'25), and 50.7 (OV-Stitcher, CVPR'26 Findings). At least 8 more training-free OVSS papers appeared at ICCV 2025 and CVPR 2026, and newer leaderboard gains are often around 1 mIoU, under evaluation protocols that differ from paper to paper. The "why does it work" question is **not settled**: papers give competing explanations (the residual/FFN path, [CLS]-induced "global patches", anomaly/high-norm tokens, sliding-window discrepancy, "lazy aggregation"), and no published study I found pits these explanations against each other under one protocol. That gap is the most defensible course-scale contribution (analysis, type D). A new training-free trick that chases SOTA has low novelty.

Releases: ViT-S/16 (21M), S+/16, B/16 (86M), L/16 (300M), H+/16 (840M), 7B/16 (6,716M), and ConvNeXt T/S/B/L. dino.txt runs on a distilled ViT-L/16 via torch.hub `dinov3_vitl16_dinotxt_tet1280d20h24l`, with a notebook for OV segmentation. The license is the custom "DINOv3 License"; **weights require a request/approval email**; code needs PyTorch ≥ 2.7.1. **[verified]** — [GitHub facebookresearch/dinov3](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3)

**Competing "why it works" explanations (the open analysis question)**
- ClearCLIP: drop the residual path and FFN in the last block and use self-self attention. ResCLIP (CVPR'25): blames last-block self-attention and adds residual attention from intermediate layers. SC-CLIP: "anomaly tokens" cause uniform attention and feature smoothing (ResNet CLIP has no anomaly tokens). FSA: refinements get lost through later projections. DIH-CLIP: underused head diversity. These are each paper's own explanation, not a consensus. **[snippet]** — [ResCLIP CVPR'25](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yang_ResCLIP_Residual_Attention_for_Training-free_Dense_Vision-language_Inference_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf), [SC-CLIP arXiv 2411.15869](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15869v1), [FSA arXiv 2508.20265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20265)
- CLIPtrase (Shao, Tian, Zhao, Su; ECCV 2024): CLIP's [CLS] token makes a few "global patches" dominate patch-patch correlations, and through softmax they "squeeze" local correlations. It claims +22.3% over CLIP on average across 9 benchmarks. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2407.08268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08268), [ECCV page](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/11599_ECCV_2024_paper.php)
- "Vision Transformers Need More Than Registers" (Cheng Shi, Yizhou Yu, Sibei Yang; arXiv 2602.22394, accepted CVPR 2026): attributes artifacts to "lazy aggregation", where background patches act as shortcuts for global semantics. It argues registers reduce high-norm tokens but **high-norm is not the root cause**, and its fix selectively integrates patch features into CLS (12 benchmarks). No code link found. **[verified abstract]** — [arXiv 2602.22394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22394)

**Evaluation-protocol inconsistency (a measurable confounder)**
- GLA-CLIP evaluates under 4 protocol "settings" (CLIP-DINOiser: 448 resize / 448 crop / 224 stride / no rename; ClearCLIP: same as CLIP-DINOiser; ProxyCLIP: 448 / 336 / 112 / rename trick; SCLIP: 336 / 224 / 112 / rename). Five datasets have an explicit background class (V21, PC60, C-Obj, City, ADE). **[verified]** — [arXiv HTML 2603.23030](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23030)
- Background handling differs across papers: a fixed 0.85 softmax threshold on VOC only in one protocol, versus ClearCLIP's dataset-specific thresholds (VOC21 0.2, PC60 0.15, COCO-Obj 0.25). One protocol uses 640×640 inputs, and FreeCP uses a 384 window with stride 112. **[snippet]** — [GLA-CLIP supp](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Lee_Looking_Beyond_the_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf), [NACLIP arXiv 2404.08181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.08181), [Trident supp](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/supplemental/Shi_Harnessing_Vision_Foundation_ICCV_2025_supplemental.pdf)

### Inferences
- [inferred] With 10+ new training-free methods per major venue and leaderboard deltas around 1 mIoU, which protocol choices alone can swing (the SCLIP avg is 38.2 vs 40.08 between two papers), a new trick is unlikely to be convincing at ICCV 2027 unless it is large (≥ +2–3 avg over Trident/OV-Stitcher-level SOTA) or comes with mechanistic insight.
- [inferred] The open, testable question: **which of the competing explanations (residual/FFN path, global patches/[CLS], high-norm outlier tokens, lazy aggregation, window discrepancy) actually accounts for the gains, and are the fixes additive or redundant?** Each paper ablates only its own fix. PH-Reg's data (Q2) already hints that "denoising artifacts" alone does little for MaskCLIP-style OVSS, which is a non-obvious result worth explaining.
- [estimate] Running an 8-benchmark eval of one ViT-B/16 training-free method on a Titan V: roughly 25k evaluation images across all splits, with 448 sliding-window crops. I estimate 0.5–2 h per full run in FP32/FP16, with peak VRAM < 4 GB at batch 1. Uncertainty is about 2×, and depends mostly on data loading and on Cityscapes/ADE resolution. Evidence that a single older 24 GB GPU suffices: GLA-CLIP ran everything on one Titan RTX. ViT-L/14 roughly triples compute.
- [inferred] Methods that need SAM (Trident) or DINO plus CLIP (ProxyCLIP, CASS) add backbones. ProxyCLIP with DINOv2 ViT-B plus CLIP ViT-B should fit in 12 GB. A Trident setup with a SAM ViT-H encoder at high resolution may be tight; use SAM ViT-B or reduce resolution. LLaVA-based "Power of Prior" (7B LLM) does not fit in FP16 on 12 GB without quantization.

### Gaps
- I could not open CVF PDFs (HTTP 403), so the PEARL, OV-Stitcher, Power-of-Prior, and S2C2Seg numbers are snippet-level only.
- SC-CLIP's venue is unverified (arXiv 2411.15869 only). NACLIP's repo owner is unverified. No official CASS code was found.
- dino.txt (CVPR 2025) title and authors were not verified on a primary page in this session (seen only in the DINOde table).
- ECCV 2026 and NeurIPS 2026 accepted training-free OVSS papers were not enumerated (proceedings listings not searched). The crowding is likely understated.
- No dedicated "protocol reproducibility" study for training-free OVSS was found. That suggests a gap, but absence in my searches is not proof.

## Q2. Seed 2 — ViT artifacts / high-norm tokens / registers, and their effect on dense tasks

### Takeaway
The register literature exploded in 2025–2026: test-time registers, post-hoc self-distilled registers, a cross-architecture reassessment, "need more than registers", an SAE analysis of register vs outlier tokens, and DiT/face/LVLM variants. The **training-free test-time registers (TTR)** of Jiang et al. (NeurIPS'25 spotlight) are cheap and have code for OpenCLIP B/16 and L/14 plus DINOv2. Two directly relevant facts set up a sharp, cheap experiment: **PH-Reg found that DVT artifact-denoising *hurts* MaskCLIP OVSS (32.21 → 30.96) and barely helps NACLIP (+0.5)**, and **nobody I found has evaluated test-time registers on the standard 8-benchmark OVSS suite or combined them with SCLIP/ClearCLIP/NACLIP.**

### Cited Findings
- "Vision Transformers Need Registers" (Darcet et al., ICLR 2024): high-norm tokens (norm > ~150 in DINOv2) appear in low-information background patches and are repurposed for global computation. Registers remove them from patch tokens. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2309.16588](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.16588)
- "Vision Transformers Don't Need Trained Registers" (Nick Jiang, Amil Dravid, Alexei Efros, Yossi Gandelsman; NeurIPS 2025 spotlight; arXiv v1 2025-06-09, v5 2025-10-25): a sparse set of "register neurons" concentrates high-norm activations on outlier tokens in CLIP and DINOv2. Shifting those activations into one extra untrained token at test time mimics trained registers, and one test-time register is enough. **[verified]** — [arXiv 2506.08010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08010), [NeurIPS poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117199). It reports about +5 mIoU on *attention-based* zero-shot segmentation and preserves zero-shot ImageNet accuracy. **[snippet]** — [NeurIPS paper PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/51bae6441380c4c4cab87f430be89ac2-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- TTR repo (github.com/nickjiang2378/test-time-registers): HF checkpoints for OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14 and LLaVA-Llama-3-8B with precomputed register neurons; DINOv2 ViT-L/14 via torch.hub; notebooks for finding register neurons. **No eval scripts in the repo.** ADE20K segmentation and NYUv2 depth eval are delegated to the Denoising-ViT repo, ImageNet zero-shot segmentation to clip_text_span. The README warns that hooks slow the forward pass. **[verified]** — [GitHub](https://github.com/nickjiang2378/test-time-registers)
- PH-Reg: "Vision Transformers with Self-Distilled Registers" (Yinjie Chen, Zipeng Yan, Chong Zhou, Bo Dai, Andrew F. Luo; NeurIPS 2025 spotlight; code github.com/0raiser0/PH-Reg). Its OVSS Table 1 (OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/16, 8-benchmark avg): MaskCLIP 32.21, **MaskCLIP+DVT 30.96**, NACLIP 39.41, **NACLIP+DVT 39.91**, SCLIP 40.08, ClearCLIP 39.52, PH-Reg 41.85. **TTR is cited only in related work, not compared.** Authors' stated limitations: reliance on test-time augmentation, sometimes underperforms DVT on DINOv2, and DVT's static-artifact assumption fits DINOv2 but not CLIP. Distillation used 4× Ada 6000 for 100 epochs, and the single-GPU DINOv2 ViT-B comparison took about 150 h (PH-Reg) vs about 365 h (DVT). **[verified]** — [arXiv HTML 2505.21501v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21501v3), [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21501)
- DVT: "Denoising Vision Transformers" (Jiawei Yang, Katie Z. Luo, et al., ECCV 2024): per-image neural-field optimization separates clean features from positional artifacts, then a lightweight block learns to predict clean features. Project page jiawei-yang.github.io/DenoisingViT. The exact GitHub URL was not confirmed from the project page ("to be updated"). **[snippet]** — [ECCV paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/11504_ECCV_2024_paper.php), [project page](https://jiawei-yang.github.io/DenoisingViT)
- SINDER: "SINDER: Repairing the Singular Defects of DINOv2" (Haoqi Wang, Tong Zhang, Mathieu Salzmann; ECCV 2024): traces artifacts to the leading left singular vector of the weights and repairs them with a smoothness-regularized fine-tune on a small dataset. Code and checkpoints at github.com/haoqiwang/sinder. **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2407.16826](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16826), [ECCV page](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/1055_ECCV_2024_paper.php)
- "Register and [CLS] tokens yield a decoupling of local and global features in large ViTs" (Alexander Lappe, Martin A. Giese; NeurIPS 2025): registers clean the attention maps, but global information becomes dominated by register content and decoupled from local features. [CLS] does the same in register-free models, so attention maps must be read with care. **[verified]** — [arXiv 2505.05892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05892)
- "Do All Vision Transformers Need Registers? A Cross-Architectural Reassessment" (Baxevanakis et al.; arXiv 2603.25803, 2026-03-26; submitted to TMLR): reproduces Darcet et al. on DINO, DINOv2, OpenCLIP, and DeiT3. Some claims do not hold for all models, and it resolves terminology inconsistencies. Per a snippet, Swin shows high-norm tokens clustered in semantically coherent patches. **[verified abstract]** — [arXiv 2603.25803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25803)
- "Decoding the Functional Roles of Register and High-Norm Patch Tokens in Vision Transformers" (Varma, Rufail, Khullar, Sharma; arXiv 2610.03698, 2026-10-02): SAEs on DINOv2 show register-token features are more semantic, while outlier-token features are more low-level/texture/background. Ablating top register features cuts representation cosine similarity by 48.17%, versus 0.31% for outlier features. **[verified]** — [arXiv 2610.03698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03698)
- Artifacts beyond DINO/CLIP: "Taming Outlier Tokens in Diffusion Transformers" (arXiv 2605.05206; per a snippet it uses SigLIP2-B as an example, finds high-norm tokens in the final layers worst at the penultimate layer, and finds that masking them does not help, implying corrupted local semantics); "Registers Matter for Pixel-Space Diffusion Transformers" (arXiv 2605.16147); "Vision Transformers for Face Recognition Need More Registers" (arXiv 2606.12036); YARD for LVLM hallucination (arXiv 2605.31429). **[snippet]** — [2605.05206](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05206v1), [2605.16147](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16147v1), [2606.12036](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12036v1), [2605.31429](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31429)
- DINOv3 counters dense-feature degradation with Gram anchoring, one stage of a 3-stage 7B training pipeline (pretrain → Gram anchoring → high-res adaptation). **[verified]** — [DINOv3 GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10104)
- I found no study measuring high-norm tokens or register effects under ImageNet-C-style corruptions or other distribution shifts. **[search result: none found]** — [search context: Darcet et al.](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2309.16588v2)

### Inferences
- [inferred] **The artifact angle is crowded as a method area** (TTR, PH-Reg, DVT, SINDER, Shi et al. CVPR'26, plus DiT/face/LVLM variants), **but crowding is low at its intersection with training-free OVSS evaluation.** The concrete unclaimed cells: TTR × {MaskCLIP, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, NACLIP, ProxyCLIP} on the 8-benchmark suite; TTR vs high-norm masking vs ClearCLIP's residual removal as competing explanations; and artifacts under corruption shift.
- [inferred] A cheap, falsifiable prediction from PH-Reg + Shi et al.: if high-norm tokens were the main reason MaskCLIP fails, TTR should recover much of the MaskCLIP→ClearCLIP gap (about 7 mIoU). If Shi et al.'s "lazy aggregation" is right, TTR alone should give small OVSS gains, while CLS/attention reweighting should matter more. Either outcome is reportable, which is good failure-tolerance for a course project.
- [estimate] TTR at inference adds one token plus forward hooks, so overhead is negligible in VRAM (< 0.5 GB extra for ViT-B/L at 448 px). Finding register neurons for a new model (e.g., OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/16, which the repo may not ship; it ships OpenCLIP) needs a pass over about 1k images, minutes on a Titan V.
- [inferred] Reproducing PH-Reg/DVT training is infeasible in 3 weeks on one Titan V (about 150–365 GPU-h on newer GPUs). Only their released checkpoints (if provided) or reported numbers can be used.

### Gaps
- Whether the TTR HF checkpoints use OpenAI CLIP weights or LAION OpenCLIP weights was not confirmed. This matters because the OVSS baselines use OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/16, and a mismatch would require running the neuron search yourself.
- Whether PH-Reg released CLIP ViT-B/16 checkpoints is unverified (repo not opened).
- SigLIP2 artifact behavior is documented only via a snippet of 2605.05206. I found no dedicated SigLIP2 register study.
- Whether the CVPR'26 "Need More Than Registers" paper evaluates OVSS is unknown (no code link; full text not read).

## Q3. Seed 3 — Robustness / generalization of foundation-model dense prediction under general domain shift (art angle dropped per scope)

### Takeaway
A ready-made, public **OVSS robustness benchmark already exists**: MLMP's suite (NeurIPS 2025) has 15 corruptions at severity 5 on VOC20/21, PC59/60, and Cityscapes, plus ACDC (fog/night/rain/snow) and GTA-V, for 87 scenarios, with code. It was built for TTA and evaluated with NACLIP as the host. **No systematic comparison of how the *training-free design families* (CLIP-attention redesign vs VFM-proxy (DINO/SAM) vs register/artifact fixes) hold up under shift was found.** This makes "does the clean leaderboard survive distribution shift, and why?" a clean analysis project with inference-only compute.

### Cited Findings
- MLMP paper: "Test-Time Adaptation of Vision-Language Models for Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation" (Mehrdad Noori, David Osowiechi, …, Ismail Ben Ayed, Christian Desrosiers; ÉTS Montréal; NeurIPS 2025). Benchmark: 9 datasets (V20, V21, P59, P60, Cityscapes, COCO-Stuff, COCO-Object, ACDC, GTA-V); 15 synthetic corruptions at severity 5 on V20/V21/P59/P60/Cityscapes; ACDC and GTA-V as real/rendered shifts; 87 scenarios. Default host is NACLIP ViT-L/14, with ViT-B/16 and B/32 in Appendix G, and SigLIP 2 and other OVSS hosts in a truncated appendix. Code: github.com/dosowiechi/MLMP. **[verified]** — [arXiv HTML 2505.21844v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v2), [NeurIPS poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/119296)
- Robustness gaps from MLMP's table (NACLIP ViT-L/14, no adaptation): V20 clean 75.91 vs V20-C avg 69.01; Cityscapes clean 29.49 vs Cityscapes-C 21.63; ACDC avg 23.35; GTA-V 25.09. **[verified]** — [arXiv HTML 2505.21844v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v2)
- Note: arXiv v1 described 7 datasets and 82 scenarios, while the NeurIPS/v2 version has 9 datasets and 87 scenarios. **[verified that the two counts conflict]** — [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v1); [v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v2)
- DAF ("Towards Continual Test-Time Adaptation of Vision-Language Models in Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation", Doloriel et al.; arXiv 2608.29923, 2026-08-30, under review; code github.com/chandlerbing65nm/DAF) evaluates on VOC20-C, Foggy Cityscapes, LoveDA, underwater, and remote sensing. It reports that entropy minimization causes patch-level class collapse. **[verified]** — [arXiv 2608.29923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29923)
- Classification-level evidence (older): DINOv2 ViT-g/14 linear probe scores 62.5 on ImageNet-Sketch vs 66.4 for OpenCLIP ViT-G/14, and DINOv2 lags weakly supervised models on -R and -Sketch. **[snippet]** — [DINOv2 arXiv 2304.07193](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07193)
- "Open-Vocabulary Domain Generalization in Urban-Scene Segmentation" (arXiv 2602.18853) exists, but it was not read. **[title only]** — [arXiv 2602.18853](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18853)

## Q4. Seed 4 — Test-time adaptation / efficient adaptation for dense tasks under shift: what fits 12 GB?

### Takeaway
TTA for OVSS fits a Titan V easily: MLMP updates only the vision LayerNorms (about 102k params) and reports about 2.1 GB peak memory on a V100 for NACLIP ViT-L/14. But the sub-area **became crowded fast in 2026**: MLMP (NeurIPS'25), Seg-TTO, TPA (Aug 2026), DAF (Aug 2026), and TMPA (CVPR'26, remote sensing). The best course angle is "reproduce & extend": test whether TTA gains are additive to training-free design gains across hosts, plus one cheap principled change. Paper-level novelty is moderate to low.

### Cited Findings
- MLMP training details: Adam with lr 1e-3, 10 iterations, batch 2, weights reset after each batch, 7 prompt templates, 224×224 input with overlapping patches for Cityscapes/ACDC. Compute: V100 32 GB; Table 7 lists about 2,094 MB peak memory, 82.4 GFLOPs, and 102,400 learnable params. The runtime figures are internally inconsistent (0.541 s adapt in the table vs "0.582 ms" in the text). Authors' limitations: layer weighting relies on entropy from a shared projection head; CLIPArTT could not run in some cases due to GPU memory; prompt gains saturate beyond 7. **[verified]** — [arXiv HTML 2505.21844v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v2)
- MLMP gains over no adaptation (NACLIP ViT-L/14): V20 75.91→83.76; V20-C 69.01→77.58; Cityscapes-C 21.63→23.02; ACDC 23.35→30.28. Baselines on V20-C: TENT 69.33, TPT 69.03, CLIPArTT 63.39, WATT 48.30. **[verified]** — [arXiv HTML 2505.21844v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v2)
- TPA: "Test-Time Prototype Adaptation for Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation" (Haozhe Wang et al.; arXiv 2608.08290, 2026-08-08; no venue). A training-free output-level plug-in that builds per-class prototypes from frozen DINO features on confident patches, using about 10% of unlabeled target images. Tested on 5 hosts, 3 CLIP backbones, and 8 benchmarks. Per a snippet: SCLIP +3.7, ClearCLIP +3.1, NACLIP +3.1 on B/16. No code link. **[verified abstract; per-host numbers snippet]** — [arXiv 2608.08290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08290), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08290)
- DAF (continual TTA, Aug 2026). See Q3. **[verified]** — [arXiv 2608.29923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29923)
- Seg-TTO (arXiv 2501.04696) adapts prompt embeddings and normalization layers with entropy objectives for domain-adaptive OVSS. TMPA (CVPR 2026) expands category names via an LLM and trains only a prompt bias with pixel entropy, for remote sensing. **[snippet]** — [Seg-TTO review](https://www.themoonlight.io/fr/review/test-time-optimization-for-domain-adaptive-open-vocabulary-segmentation), [TMPA notes](https://en.papernotes.org/CVPR2026/segmentation/test-time_multi-prompt_adaptation_for_open-vocabulary_remote_sensing_image_segme/)

### Inferences
- [inferred] Untested by others, as far as I found: (H-T1) TTA gain shrinks as the host gets stronger (MaskCLIP > NACLIP > ProxyCLIP > Trident), meaning TTA and training-free redesign partly fix the same deficiency. (H-T2) Entropy-based TTA on CLIP hosts is destabilized by outlier/high-norm tokens, since their near-uniform attention produces confident-but-wrong patches. Adding TTR before TTA, or excluding outlier tokens from the loss, should reduce collapse. DAF's report of entropy-induced patch collapse makes H-T2 plausible but untested.
- [estimate] Running MLMP-style TTA on ViT-B/16 at 448 px with sliding windows: under 4 GB VRAM. ViT-L/14: under 8 GB. FP32 is fine, so no bf16 is needed. Runtime is about 10× inference (10 adaptation steps), so a full 8-benchmark host×TTA sweep takes about 10–20 GPU-h per host. Subsample.
- [inferred] LoRA/linear-probe adaptation of frozen DINOv2/v3 ViT-B to Cityscapes and testing on ACDC is easily feasible (feature caching), but it is a well-trodden domain-generalized segmentation setting with low novelty. It works as a baseline, not as the project.

### Gaps
- TPA and DAF are recent preprints whose numbers are unreviewed. TPA has no code.
- Domain-generalized segmentation with VFMs (e.g., parameter-efficient DG methods for Cityscapes→ACDC) was not surveyed in this session.

## Q5. Concrete candidate project directions (2–3), with problem, ideas, deliverables, data, compute, value, risks

### Takeaway
**Rank 1 (recommended): Candidate A**, a controlled analysis of what training-free OVSS fixes actually fix: outlier/high-norm tokens vs residual/FFN path vs [CLS]/global-patch dominance. It uses test-time registers as a new intervention. It is cheap (inference only), has clear baselines and metrics, and gives a meaningful result even if the hypothesis fails. **Rank 2: Candidate B**, a robustness analysis of training-free OVSS families under corruption, ACDC, and GTA-V shift. It reuses the MLMP benchmark and can be merged with A as one project with two axes. **Rank 3: Candidate C**, TTA × training-free host additivity, plus a register/outlier-aware TTA tweak. Feasible, but the area is crowded. All three share one codebase (an MMSeg-style eval of the SCLIP/ClearCLIP/NACLIP family), so the student can pivot between them.

### Cited Findings (anchors used by the candidates)
- Baseline numbers and protocols to reproduce: MaskCLIP 32.21, SCLIP 40.08, ClearCLIP 39.52, NACLIP 39.41 avg (OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/16; PH-Reg protocol). — [PH-Reg HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21501v3). SCLIP 38.2 / ClearCLIP 38.1 / NACLIP 39.4 / ProxyCLIP 42.3 / CASS 44.4 (GLA-CLIP protocols). — [GLA-CLIP HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23030)
- MaskCLIP+DVT is worse than MaskCLIP (30.96 vs 32.21), and NACLIP+DVT gains only +0.5. — [PH-Reg HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21501v3)
- TTR code covers OpenCLIP B/16 and L/14 plus DINOv2 and ships notebooks to find register neurons. — [TTR GitHub](https://github.com/nickjiang2378/test-time-registers)
- Competing explanations to test: CLIPtrase (global patches via [CLS]) — [arXiv 2407.08268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08268); SC-CLIP (anomaly tokens) — [arXiv 2411.15869](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15869v1); "Need More Than Registers" (lazy aggregation; high-norm not root cause) — [arXiv 2602.22394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22394); Lappe & Giese (register/[CLS] decouple local and global) — [arXiv 2505.05892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05892)
- Robustness benchmark and code: MLMP suite (15 corruptions, ACDC, GTA-V; 87 scenarios). — [arXiv 2505.21844v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v2)

### Inferences — Candidate A: "What do training-free OVSS fixes actually fix? Outlier tokens vs attention-path redesign" (type D analysis, plus a small type-A method if a finding suggests one)
- **Input → Output:** an image and a set of class names → a per-pixel class map from frozen CLIP (OpenAI ViT-B/16; ViT-L/14 secondary), *plus* diagnostic measurements (token norms, attention mass on outlier tokens, attention entropy/locality, patch-text similarity maps).
- **Research question:** How much of the MaskCLIP→{SCLIP, ClearCLIP, NACLIP} gain is explained by neutralizing high-norm/outlier tokens, versus changing the attention aggregation (q-q/k-k/self-self, neighborhood prior), versus removing the residual/FFN path? Are these fixes redundant or additive?
- **Interventions (factorial, all training-free):** host ∈ {MaskCLIP, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, NACLIP, (ProxyCLIP)} × artifact fix ∈ {none, test-time register (Jiang et al.), zero out register neurons, mask top-k high-norm tokens out of last-block attention, [CLS]-attention removal à la CLIPtrase}. Repeat the norm diagnostics on DINOv2 (with and without trained registers) as the proxy backbone in ProxyCLIP.
- **Hypotheses (falsifiable):** (H1) TTR improves MaskCLIP but recovers < 50% of the MaskCLIP→ClearCLIP gap. This prediction is consistent with PH-Reg's DVT result and with Shi et al. (H2) TTR gains are near zero on ClearCLIP/SCLIP (redundant: they already bypass the path where outliers dominate) but positive on NACLIP/ProxyCLIP. (H3) Per-image mIoU gain correlates with the image's outlier-token attention mass (Spearman ρ > 0.3); if not, outliers are not the mechanism. Failure criterion: if TTR gives > 70% of ClearCLIP's gain, the "attention redesign" story is the weaker one, which is a publishable surprise in the other direction.
- **Minimum 3-week deliverable:** (1) reproduce 4 baselines under **one** fixed protocol and report the deviation from published numbers; (2) a factorial table on 8 benchmarks (or a 5-benchmark subset if time is short: V21, PC59, C-Stf, City, ADE); (3) diagnostic plots (norm histograms, attention-to-outlier mass, a per-image gain vs outlier-mass scatter); (4) qualitative maps and failure cases (small objects, stuff classes, background thresholding); (5) ablations of the number of test-time registers, the layer at which to intervene, and the high-norm threshold.
- **Datasets/metrics:** VOC 2012 (V21/V20), PASCAL Context (PC60/PC59), COCO-Stuff/COCO-Object, Cityscapes, ADE20K; mIoU. Optional: Boundary Error Rate from GLA-CLIP. Download sizes from memory, **[unverified]**: VOC2012 about 2 GB; ADE20K val zip about 1 GB; COCO val2017 about 1 GB plus stuff annotations; Cityscapes gtFine plus leftImg8bit about 11 GB (registration required).
- **Difficulty:** low to medium. Hooking CLIP's last blocks and porting TTR to OpenAI CLIP is the main engineering, which may require re-running the neuron search. **VRAM [estimate]:** < 4 GB for ViT-B/16 at 448 px, < 8 GB for ViT-L/14. **Runtime [estimate]:** 0.5–2 h per 8-benchmark run. About 20 runs come to roughly 1–2 days of GPU time.
- **Learning value:** high (ViT internals, attention mechanics, controlled experiments, interpretability). **Career value:** good. Mechanistic analysis of foundation models is a sought-after skill.
- **ICCV-2027 potential (honest):** the course-level contribution is a careful factorial reproduction plus diagnostics. Paper-level novelty would need either (a) a surprising, robust finding that unifies or overturns several published explanations across CLIP, SigLIP2, and DINOv3 dino.txt and multiple sizes, or (b) a principled method derived from it that beats strong SOTA (Trident/OV-Stitcher level, about 48–51 avg) or explains why the SOTA works. **Closest existing works:** PH-Reg (NeurIPS'25; registers × OVSS, but via distillation and without TTR), SC-CLIP (anomaly tokens in OVSS), CLIPtrase (global patches), and Shi et al. CVPR'26 (high-norm is not the root cause). The novelty window is narrow, and someone may publish TTR × OVSS at any time.
- **Risks:** a negative or flat result (TTR does nothing). That is still reportable as analysis, but less exciting. Porting TTR to OpenAI CLIP weights is needed if the repo only covers LAION OpenCLIP. Protocol mismatch leads to unreproduced baseline numbers. Mitigation: fix a single protocol (e.g., the SCLIP setting) and report published-vs-reproduced numbers side by side.

### Inferences — Candidate B: "Does the training-free OVSS leaderboard survive distribution shift?" (type D)
- **Input → Output:** a corrupted, adverse-weather, or synthetic image and class names → a segmentation map. The analysis output is robustness curves, rank stability, and an attribution to design components.
- **Hypotheses:** H-R1 to H-R3 in Q3. Main metrics: mIoU under shift, relative robustness (mIoU_shift / mIoU_clean), and Kendall τ between clean and shifted rankings.
- **Hosts:** MaskCLIP, SCLIP, ClearCLIP, NACLIP (CLIP-attention family); ProxyCLIP, plus Trident (or CASS if code exists) for the VFM-proxy family; each with and without TTR (links to Candidate A).
- **Data:** the MLMP benchmark (V20-C, V21-C, P59-C, Cityscapes-C at severity 5; ACDC; GTA-V). Code: github.com/dosowiechi/MLMP.
- **Minimum deliverable:** about 6 hosts × (5 representative corruptions × 3 datasets + ACDC) table, rank-stability analysis, qualitative degradation examples, and a failure taxonomy (e.g., which classes collapse to background under fog or night).
- **VRAM/runtime [estimate]:** inference only; ≤ 6 GB with SAM ViT-B in Trident; about 10–20 GPU-h for the subsampled grid.
- **Novelty:** course level: solid. Paper level: low on its own, since benchmark-only papers need breadth (more models such as SigLIP2, DINOv3 dino.txt, and Talk2DINO; more shifts) or a mechanistic explanation. It becomes stronger when merged with Candidate A ("artifacts/aggregation as the mechanism behind robustness differences").

### Inferences — Candidate C: "Are test-time adaptation and training-free redesign complementary? (+ outlier-aware TTA)" (type C reproduce & extend, small type A)
- **Input → Output:** an unlabeled test image (or stream) plus class names → a segmentation map after per-image LayerNorm adaptation.

## Q6. Compute and Volta (Titan V, sm_70) feasibility

### Takeaway
All three candidates are **inference-dominated** and fit 12 GB at ViT-B/16 (and mostly ViT-L/14). The real Volta risk is **software, not memory**. PyTorch dropped Volta from its CUDA 12.8/12.9 builds in 2.11 and announced the end of its CUDA 12.6 builds (the last ones supporting Maxwell, Pascal, and Volta) from 2.15. CUDA 13.x does not support < sm_75. Pin a cu126 (or older cu118/cu121) environment. MMSeg/mmcv-based training-free repos usually expect older torch versions anyway.

### Cited Findings
- PyTorch 2.8: Maxwell and Pascal were dropped from the CUDA 12.8/12.9 builds. PyTorch 2.11: Volta (sm_70) was dropped from the CUDA 12.8/12.9 builds, and the PyPI default moved to CUDA 13.0. The CUDA 12.6 wheels remain the Volta option. The official announcement says CUDA 12.6 wheels will no longer be published from PyTorch 2.15, ending prebuilt Maxwell/Pascal/Volta support. CUDA 13.x does not support compute capability < 7.5. — [PyTorch dev-discuss: dropping Volta from CUDA 12.8 builds (2.11)](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/dropping-volta-support-from-cuda-12-8-binaries-for-release-2-11/3290), [Notice: CUDA 12.6 wheels end from 2.15](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/notice-cuda-12-6-wheels-will-no-longer-be-published-from-pytorch-2-15-drops-maxwell-pascal-volta/3432), [CUDA 13.2 / deprecating 12.8 (2.12)](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/introducing-cuda-13-2-and-deprecating-cuda-12-8-release-2-12/3337), [PyTorch 2.8 notes](https://newreleases.io/project/pypi/torch/release/2.8.0)

- MLMP TTA peak memory is about 2.1 GB (NACLIP ViT-L/14, V100). — [arXiv HTML 2505.21844v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21844v2)
- GLA-CLIP ran all its training-free OVSS experiments on a single Titan RTX. — [arXiv HTML 2603.23030](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23030)

### Inferences
- [inferred] Recommended environment: one conda env with torch 2.1–2.4 + cu118/cu121 + mmcv 2.x + mmsegmentation 1.x for the SCLIP/ClearCLIP/NACLIP/ProxyCLIP family, since mmcv prebuilt wheels lag torch releases (from background knowledge; **[unverified]** for the exact version matrix). PyTorch SDPA falls back to math or memory-efficient kernels on sm_70.

## Q7. Directions considered and rejected (with reasons)

### Takeaway
Rejected: (1) a new SOTA training-free OVSS trick, because the field is saturated and gains are protocol-sensitive; (2) training or reproducing post-hoc register methods (PH-Reg/DVT), at 150–365 GPU-h; (3) DiT/generative-model artifact analysis, which is off focus, already claimed in 2026, and heavy on Volta without bf16; (4) SAE/interpretability of register tokens, already done in Oct 2026; (5) LoRA/linear-probe domain generalization, low novelty; (6) DINOv3/dino.txt-centric OVSS training, gated weights and crowded; (7) culture-tech/art and 3D/VGGT, excluded by user scope.

### Cited Findings
- (1) Saturation: 8-benchmark avg about 38 → 44.4 → 48.6 → 50.7 over 2024–2026, with many CVPR'26 and ICCV'25 entries. — [GLA-CLIP HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23030), [Trident arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09219), [OV-Stitcher CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026F/html/Moon_OV-Stitcher_A_Global_Context-Aware_Framework_for_Training-Free_Open_Vocabulary_Semantic_CVPRF_2026_paper.html)

- (3) DiT outlier-token papers already exist (2605.05206, 2605.16147). — [2605.05206](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05206v1), [2605.16147](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16147v1)

- (6) DINOv3 weights require an access request, and Talk2DINO/DINOde already cover DINOv3-based OVSS. — [DINOv3 GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3), [DINOde arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21371)
- (7) For the record only (excluded by scope): the art-segmentation dataset DRAM (Cohen, Newman, Shamir; Eurographics/CGF 2022) has 718 labeled test paintings, 11 VOC classes plus background, and is research-only. It is also part of MESS. — [arXiv 2203.03238](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03238), [DRAM page](https://faculty.runi.ac.il/arik/site/artseg/Dram-Dataset.html)

### Inferences
- [inferred] SINDER-style repair fine-tuning (ECCV'24) is feasible only for smaller DINOv2 sizes on 12 GB, and the artifact-repair method space is already crowded. Treat it as a related-work comparison, not a project.
- [inferred] "The Power of Prior" (LLaVA-based training-free OVSS, CVPR'26) needs a 7B LLM, which is infeasible in FP16 on 12 GB and only possible with 4-bit quantization, which changes the method. Reject.
- [inferred] Talk2DINO-style mapping training (light MLP on frozen backbones) is computationally feasible. It is a viable *backup* host for Candidate A/B (a DINO-based OVSS with or without registers), but not a standalone project, given DINOde and dino.txt.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether any ECCV 2026 paper already does "robustness of training-free OVSS" or "registers × training-free OVSS". This is the most important pre-proposal check.
