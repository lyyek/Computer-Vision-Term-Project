## Candidate A — "Do training-free LVLM hallucination fixes improve grounding, or only shift the operating point?" (analysis of VLM object hallucination; types D + C)

### Takeaway
New training-free *methods* for object hallucination are saturated. An unofficial index lists 33 CVPR 2026 hallucination papers, 21 of them training-free decoding or attention interventions. A rigorous *evaluation/analysis* project is still well-motivated, cheap on 12 GB, and backed by public benchmarks and code. A NeurIPS 2025 paper showed that the POPE gains of contrastive decoding are largely an artifact of the operating point. Nobody has verifiably done the same audit for the attention-intervention family (PAI, VAR, ICT, ACG-style methods) under length- and threshold-controlled metrics, though I did not exhaustively check this. That makes this the safest course project in this area, with strong evaluation clarity. It is paper-level only if the audit produces a general, surprising finding plus a protocol others would adopt.

### Cited Findings
**Problem (Input → Output).** Input: an image plus a yes/no probe ("Is there a {object} in the image?") or a captioning prompt. Output: an answer or caption. The research object is a family of inference-time interventions, and the measured quantity is hallucination under a controlled protocol. [Inferred framing]

- **Critique paper (central to this candidate)**: "The Mirage of Performance Gains: Why Contrastive Decoding Fails to Mitigate Object Hallucinations in MLLMs?", Hao Yin, Guangzong Si, Zilei Wang. arXiv 2504.10020 (v1 14 Apr 2025, v4 4 Jun 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10020). NeurIPS 2025 poster — [NeurIPS 2025 virtual](https://nips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/115342). Claims [Verified abstract]: POPE gains of contrastive decoding come from (i) crude unidirectional output-distribution adjustments and (ii) the adaptive plausibility constraint degrading sampling to near-greedy search. Critiqued methods include VCD, ICD and SID [Secondary] — [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/the-mirage-of-performance-gains-why-contrastive-decoding-fails-to-address-multimodal-hallucination). **Benchmarks, data and evaluation tooling**
- POPE official repo: [GitHub RUCAIBox/POPE](https://github.com/RUCAIBox/POPE) (last push 2025-08) [Verified exists]. AMBER (LLM-free, multi-dimensional) official repo: [GitHub junyangwang0410/AMBER](https://github.com/junyangwang0410/AMBER) [Verified exists]
- POPE is binary yes/no, so greedy decoding is favored and gains can be "deceptive" when a method collapses sampling to greedy. This is the Mirage argument [Secondary] — [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/the-mirage-of-performance-gains-why-contrastive-decoding-fails-to-address-multimodal-hallucination)
- CHAIR versus recall trade-off evidence from the pruning literature: attention-based token selection lowers CHAIR (C_S, C_I) but also lowers recall. This shows that CHAIR alone can reward less-detailed captions [Secondary/preprint] — [AgilePruner arXiv 2603.01236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01236)

**Limitations stated by authors versus inferred**
- Stated (Mirage): contrastive-decoding POPE gains are not hallucination-related; spurious baselines match them [Verified abstract] — [arXiv 2504.10020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10020)
- Stated (VAR): sink tokens carry "surplus" attention, and the method only redistributes within image-centric heads [Verified] — [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/da8a39bc39ae1c89dd6ebb1e3bcbb3f3-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Inferred (not stated by those authors): attention-amplification methods (PAI, VAR, ICT-style) could reduce CHAIR mainly by shortening captions or making them more conservative, and could raise POPE accuracy mainly by shifting the yes-ratio. Neither effect would show up in single-number accuracy or CHAIR reports.

### Inferences
- **Saturation verdict [Inferred]:** proposing another decoding or attention intervention would be a "small variant". With about 21 training-free CVPR 2026 entries plus a continuous arXiv stream, it is not paper-level novel. The open niche is *measurement*: Mirage covered only contrastive decoding on POPE, so attention-intervention methods and generative (CHAIR/AMBER) metrics remain unaudited, as far as I could verify.
- **Concrete testable hypotheses [Hypothesis]:**
  - H1 (threshold-free POPE): compute AUROC/AP from P("Yes") instead of argmax accuracy. Most of the POPE gain of VCD/PAI/VAR then shrinks substantially. Matching each method's yes-ratio to the baseline (logit bias calibration) closes most of the accuracy gap.
  - H2 (length-controlled CHAIR): plot CHAIR_i against object recall (or against caption length via max_new_tokens sweeps or an EOS-bias sweep). Attention-amplification methods then lie on or near the baseline's trade-off curve rather than strictly dominating it.
  - H3 (mechanism): the image-attention mass of hallucinated object tokens is lower than that of correct object tokens, and it decays with token position. Interventions that help should raise image-attention specifically at hallucinated positions, not uniformly. This is testable with per-token CHAIR labels.
  - Spurious-baseline control (reusing Mirage's idea): a pure "No"-bias logit shift, an attention scaling on *random* heads, and caption-length truncation. If these match a method on a metric, that metric does not show grounding for that method.
- **Minimum deliverable by 2026-12-06 [Estimate]:** baseline plus 3 methods (VCD, PAI, VAR; OPERA on a subset only) on LLaVA-1.5-7B (NF4) and a second model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B fp16 or a ~2B InternVL). Metrics: POPE-COCO 3 splits with acc/F1/yes-ratio plus AUROC; CHAIR on about 500 COCO val images with length-controlled curves; AMBER discriminative subset if time allows. Also 2 spurious baselines, a per-head attention figure, and a failure-case gallery. This meets the course's requirements for fair baselines, ablations and failure analysis.
- **Success/failure criteria [Inferred]:** both outcomes are reportable. If gains survive AUROC and recall-matching, that is positive evidence that attention interventions (unlike contrastive decoding) truly ground. If they vanish, it extends Mirage. This is valuable because a "negative" result is still a result.
- **VRAM/runtime [Estimate, uncertain ±2×]:** LLaVA-1.5-7B in NF4 needs about 4–5 GB of weights plus about 1–2 GB of activations for 576 visual tokens. Qwen2.5-VL-3B in fp16 needs about 7.5 GB of weights; cap max_pixels so there are about 256–400 visual tokens. POPE (about 9k single-token answers across 3 COCO splits) should take roughly 0.5–1.5 GPU-h per method per model. VCD costs about 2× (two passes). CHAIR at 500 captions × ≤512 tokens should take roughly 1.5–4 GPU-h per configuration. OPERA beam search is several times slower, so restrict it to about 100 images. The full matrix (2 models × 5 methods × 2 benchmarks plus sweeps) should fit in about 40–80 GPU-h, which is feasible in 3 weeks on 1 GPU if the run list is fixed early.
- **Volta-specific risks [Verified facts + Inferred impact]:** see the cross-cutting Volta section. Key points: LLaVA-1.5-7B does not fit in fp16 (about 14 GB) and LLM.int8 is unsupported on sm_70, so only NF4 4-bit is possible. Quantization may itself change hallucination rates, so the baseline and every method must use the same quantized model, and published numbers are not directly comparable. Qwen2.x-VL has documented fp16 attention overflow.
- **Learning/career value [Inferred]:** high. It teaches LVLM internals (attention hooks, logits, KV cache), benchmark hygiene, statistical evaluation and mechanistic analysis. These skills transfer directly to VLM industry and research roles.
- **ICCV 2027 potential [Inferred]:** moderate-low as-is, and moderate if extended. A paper-level version would need (a) multiple model families including recent ones (Qwen3-VL class), (b) a principled protocol (threshold-free, length-matched, spurious-controlled) applied to about 10 methods including CVPR 2026 ones, and (c) a mechanistic explanation. The existence of Mirage at NeurIPS 2025 shows that venues accept evaluation critiques, but it is also the closest prior work, and reviewers will ask "what is new beyond Mirage?"
- **Main risks [Inferred]:** (1) someone has already published a CHAIR length or recall audit of attention methods (check before the proposal; see Gaps). (2) Reproduction drift: re-implementing PAI/VAR on Qwen2.5-VL may not match the papers' LLaVA-1.5 numbers. (3) The NF4 confound.

## Candidate B — "When does inversion-free flow editing fail?" Analysis plus a training-free extension of FlowEdit on PIE-Bench (diffusion/flow analysis; types C + D)

### Takeaway
Among the generation and editing directions, training-free *text-based image editing* has much clearer evaluation than style transfer. PIE-Bench offers 700 images, 10 edit types, edit masks and a ready evaluation script. FlowEdit (ICCV 2025) is a strong, public, model-agnostic baseline whose authors explicitly state its limitations. FlowEdit itself was *not* evaluated on PIE-Bench. A controlled PIE-Bench analysis of where it fails (edit-region size, edit type, n_max) plus one training-free adaptive variant is a feasible 3-week reproduce-and-extend project on SD3-medium. The main risks are 2026 crowding (SNR-Edit, EKFEdit, UniEdit-Flow, FlowBypass) and slow 1024² inference on Volta.

### Cited Findings
**Problem (Input → Output).** Input: a real image, a source prompt and a target prompt. Output: an edited image that follows the target text and preserves the unedited structure and background. No training and no inversion. [Inferred framing consistent with the paper]

**Key papers**
- **FlowEdit**: "FlowEdit: Inversion-Free Text-Based Editing Using Pre-Trained Flow Models", Vladimir Kulikov, Matan Kleiner, Inbar Huberman-Spiegelglas, Tomer Michaeli. ICCV 2025 (pp. 19721–19730), with an ICCV oral listing — [CVF Open Access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Kulikov_FlowEdit_Inversion-Free_Text-Based_Editing_Using_Pre-Trained_Flow_Models_ICCV_2025_paper.html); [ICCV oral page](https://iccv.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/oral/2925); [arXiv 2412.08629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08629v1). Code: [GitHub fallenshock/FlowEdit](https://github.com/fallenshock/FlowEdit) (1,025 stars, last push 2026-08) [Verified]
  - Evaluation [Verified from the arXiv v2 HTML]: 70+ real 1024×1024 images (DIV2K, Pexels, PxHere) and 250+ text-image pairs. **Not PIE-Bench.** Metrics: LPIPS (structure) and CLIP-T, with CLIP-I, DINO and DreamSim in the appendix, plus FID/KID for a 1,000-image cat→dog study. Baselines: ODE inversion, SDEdit, iRFDS (SD3), RF-Inversion and RF Edit (FLUX). Diffusion-model editors were not compared ("unfair"), and Stable Flow was not run ("did not fit on their GPU"). Settings: SD3-medium with T=50, n_max=33, CFG 3.5/13.5; FLUX.1-dev with T=28, n_max=24, CFG 1.5/5.5. Hardware: RTX A6000. No runtime or memory reported — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08629v2)
  - **Author-stated limitations (quoted)**: FlowEdit is "often limited in its ability to make substantial modifications to large regions of the image", and "our method is based on a heuristic, which is not guaranteed to precisely map to the target distribution." Shown failure cases: pose and background edits (Fig. S6), and extra-leg artifacts from random noise (Fig. S8) — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08629v2) [Verified]
- **PIE-Bench / PnP Inversion**: "PnP Inversion: Boosting Diffusion-based Editing with 3 Lines of Code", ICLR 2024. Official repo — [GitHub cure-lab/PnPInversion](https://github.com/cure-lab/PnPInversion) [Verified]. PIE-Bench has 700 images and 10 edit categories (random, change object, add, delete, change content, pose, color, material, background, style). Categories 1–9 are split between artificial and natural images. Annotations include source and target prompts, an instruction, blended words and an RLE edit **mask**. Access is via a Google Form. `evaluation/evaluate.py` computes structure distance; PSNR, LPIPS, MSE and SSIM on the unedited region; and CLIP similarity (whole and edited region). The repo also includes P2P (DDIM, null-text, negative-prompt, DirectInversion), MasaCtrl, PnP, Pix2Pix-Zero, EDICT, InstructPix2Pix and others — [PnPInversion README](https://github.com/cure-lab/PnPInversion) [Verified]. No licence is shown on the page.

**2025–2026 follow-ups (crowding)**

### Inferences
- **Hypotheses [Hypothesis]:**
  - H1: FlowEdit's CLIP-edited-region score falls and background preservation rises as PIE-Bench mask area grows, and it fails disproportionately on "change pose" and "change background". This quantifies the authors' stated limitation per category.
  - H2: the best n_max (how early editing starts) depends on the edit. Large-area or semantic edits need larger n_max; color and material edits need smaller n_max. A training-free per-image adaptive rule then dominates the best fixed n_max on the structure-versus-CLIP Pareto front. The rule could use mask area estimated from source/target cross-attention, or the magnitude of the early velocity difference V_tar − V_src.
  - H3 (analysis): FlowEdit's noise-averaging (n_avg) mainly reduces artifacts (e.g., extra limbs) rather than improving text adherence. This is testable as an artifact rate against n_avg.
- **Minimum deliverable [Estimate]:** FlowEdit, ODE inversion and SDEdit on SD3-medium (T5 dropped or precomputed) over a **stratified PIE-Bench subset** (e.g., 20 images per category = 200), using the official PIE-Bench metrics. Then a per-category and mask-area breakdown, an n_max/n_avg sweep (ablations), one adaptive-schedule variant, and a failure gallery. Optionally, a full 700-image run of the final configuration. Optional SD1.5 reference points come from PnPInversion's methods (P2P + DirectInversion), which run easily on 12 GB.
- **VRAM/runtime [Estimate, high uncertainty]:** SD3-medium MMDiT (~2B) fp16 needs about 4 GB; the CLIP-L and OpenCLIP-bigG encoders about 1.5 GB; the VAE is small. T5-XXL (about 9.5 GB fp16) does not fit alongside, so either drop it or precompute T5 embeddings for all ~1,400 prompts once on CPU in fp32 (needs ≳20 GB system RAM) and cache them. Each FlowEdit image at 1024² runs about 33 steps × (source + target) × CFG ≈ 130 transformer passes, roughly 1–3 min per image on a Titan V. So 200 images × 1 config ≈ 3–10 GPU-h, and a sweep of about 10 configs is 30–100 GPU-h. This is tight, so run sweeps at 512–768² or on fewer images. PIE-Bench images are, I believe, 512×512 (unverified) while FlowEdit/SD3 ran at 1024²; the resolution mismatch must be handled and stated.
- **Volta-specific risks [Inferred]:** SD3/SD3.5 fp16 inference is the documented diffusers path, so it is likely fine. FLUX.1-dev (12B) is not feasible in fp16 on 12 GB (about 24 GB of weights), and NF4 would be slow and would add a quantization confound, so stay on SD3-medium. The 8-bit-T5 path in diffusers relies on bitsandbytes LLM.int8, which needs CC 7.5+ and does not work on Volta.
- **Licensing/access [Inferred]:** the SD3/SD3.5-medium HF repos are gated (the model card requires agreeing to share contact information — [HF SD3.5-medium](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-3.5-medium)). Request access before 11-18. PIE-Bench access goes through a Google Form, so request it immediately.
- **Learning/career value [Inferred]:** high. It covers rectified-flow/ODE sampling, CFG, editing theory (inversion versus direct ODE), benchmark-based evaluation, and Pareto reporting for generative models.
- **ICCV 2027 potential [Inferred]:** moderate. A PIE-Bench re-evaluation is a course-level contribution. An adaptive-schedule rule could be a paper component only if it generalizes across SD3 and FLUX (FLUX would need more GPU memory later) and clearly beats SNR-Edit and EKFEdit. The space is crowded with 2026 FlowEdit follow-ups.
- **Main risks [Inferred]:** (1) compute time at 1024²; (2) the T5-drop confound (prompt adherence differs from the paper); (3) PIE-Bench CLIP metrics are coarse, and structure distance and CLIP trade off, so single numbers mislead and Pareto curves must be reported; (4) SNR-Edit may already cover region-aware correction.

- The PIE-Bench numbers reported by SNR-Edit, EKFEdit, UniEdit-Flow and FlowBypass were not read, so no "number to beat" is available here.

## Candidate C — Visual token pruning for efficient VLMs: saturation verdict, plus a "pruning × hallucination" probe as a safe extension of Candidate A

### Takeaway
Visual token pruning is **heavily saturated**. An unofficial CVPR 2026 index lists about 30 token-compression papers in one "VLM efficiency" category (14 explicitly training-free). Arxiv carries a steady 2026 stream, including several "rethinking" papers. A new pruning method has low paper-level novelty. As a course project it is safe because evaluation is clean (accuracy against token budget and latency) and the code is public. The most defensible angle is an analysis that reuses Candidate A's infrastructure: does pruning change hallucination and grounding, and is that just a caption-length or recall effect? Existing preprints (AgilePruner, VASparse, MINT, PruneHal) already partly pre-empt this.

### Cited Findings
**Problem (Input → Output).** Input: an image and a prompt to a VLM with N visual tokens. Output: the same task answer using K ≪ N tokens, with minimal accuracy loss and real latency or FLOPs savings. [Inferred framing]

**Key papers**
- **FastV**: "An Image is Worth 1/2 Tokens After Layer 2: Plug-and-Play Inference Acceleration for Large Vision-Language Models", Chen et al. (author from memory), **ECCV 2024 Oral** — [GitHub pkunlp-icler/FastV](https://github.com/pkunlp-icler/FastV) (601 stars) [Verified repo/venue]
- **SparseVLM**, Zhang et al. (author from memory), **ICML 2025**, with an extended SparseVLM+ in TPAMI 2026 per the repo — [GitHub Gumpest/SparseVLMs](https://github.com/Gumpest/SparseVLMs) (last push 2026-07) [Verified repo/venue claims]
- **VisionZip**, Yang et al. (author from memory), **CVPR 2025** — [GitHub JIA-Lab-research/VisionZip](https://github.com/JIA-Lab-research/VisionZip) (moved from dvlab-research; 464 stars) [Verified repo/venue]
- **Critique**: "Token Pruning in Multimodal Large Language Models: Are We Solving the Right Problem?", Zichen Wen, Yifeng Gao, Weijia Li, Conghui He, Linfeng Zhang, ACL 2025 Findings — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.802/); [arXiv 2502.11501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11501). Findings [Verified via arXiv HTML + Secondary]: many methods underperform random selection. LLM-internal text-visual attention (FastV, SparseVLM) may not reflect true visual relevance, and attention-based methods favor tokens near the end of the visual sequence. Language guidance helps only for strongly language-tied tasks. Random and pooling methods preserve spatial coverage. FLOPs are an unreliable proxy for speedup, so measure latency — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11501v2); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/token-pruning-in-multimodal-large-language-models-are-we-solving-the-right-problem)

**Crowding evidence (2026)**
- The CVPR 2026 "VLM efficiency" category on an unofficial index has **63** papers, about **30** clearly on visual token pruning, compression or merging, and **14** explicitly training-free. Examples include "When Token Pruning is Worse than Random: Understanding Visual Token Information in VLLMs", "CoIn", "SCoRe", "Hi-Lo Prune", "TransPrune", "ZOO-Prune", "HAWK", "IF-Prune" and "Rethinking Token Reduction for LVLMs" [Secondary] — [papernotes CVPR2026/vlm_efficiency](https://en.papernotes.org/CVPR2026/vlm_efficiency/)

- One search surfaced at least 9 more 2026 arXiv pruning papers, e.g., LearnPruner ([arXiv 2604.23950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23950)), CoRePrune "From Token Importance to Conditional Removability" ([arXiv 2609.26484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26484v1)), "Who Speaks for the Pruned? … Coverage Optimization" ([arXiv 2609.03158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03158)), ERA ([arXiv 2606.31982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31982)) and "Reroute, Don't Remove" ([arXiv 2606.12412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12412)) [Verified existence; LearnPruner's claimed ICLR 2026 venue is unverified]

**Pruning × hallucination (closest works to the proposed angle)**
- AgilePruner (arXiv 2603.01236): higher attention-based selection reduces CHAIR hallucination but lowers recall. Diversity-oriented pruning correlates with more hallucination. Higher-rank methods do better on POPE — [arXiv 2603.01236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01236) [Secondary/preprint]
- VASparse (arXiv 2501.06553): vision-agnostic sparsification (FastV, SparseVLM) "tends to worsen visual fuzziness and exacerbate" hallucination — [arXiv 2501.06553](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.06553) [Secondary]
- MINT (arXiv 2502.00717) reports a small POPE gain on LLaVA with 432/576 tokens (82.38 versus 82.13) — [arXiv 2502.00717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00717). PruneHal uses adaptive KV-cache pruning as a mitigation — [arXiv 2510.19183](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19183v1). A paper on Qwen2.5-VL reports that most compression baselines noticeably degrade POPE — [arXiv 2607.22716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.22716) [Secondary]

### Inferences
- **Verdict [Inferred]:** not recommended as a *new-method* project (dozens of near-duplicates). Acceptable as (a) a safe fallback or (b) an add-on ablation to Candidate A: "Under threshold-free POPE and recall-matched CHAIR, do FastV, VisionZip, random and pooling at matched budgets (64/128/192 tokens) differ in grounding, or only in caption length?" AgilePruner already reports the CHAIR–recall trade-off, so the new piece would only be the matched-operating-point protocol, which is course-level.
- **Volta-specific note [Inferred]:** FastV and SparseVLM need explicit attention maps, so they need eager attention. Volta has no FlashAttention-2 anyway, so implementation is easy, but **latency numbers measured on Titan V with eager attention are not representative** of modern FA2/FA3 deployments. Report FLOPs and latency with caveats, as the ACL Findings critique recommends.
- **Compute [Estimate]:** the same as Candidate A (LLaVA-1.5-7B NF4 / Qwen2.5-VL-3B fp16). Pruning lowers cost. A standard multi-benchmark sweep (GQA, MME, POPE, TextVQA subsets) across 4 methods × 3 budgets is about 20–40 GPU-h.
- **ICCV 2027 potential [Inferred]:** low. Learning value is medium-high (VLM internals, efficiency measurement).

## Seed direction 3 revisited: training-free style injection and other diffusion directions (general CV, folk-art angle removed). Evaluation verdict

### Takeaway
Training-free style injection (StyleID/StyleAligned/InstantStyle family) runs easily on 12 GB with SD1.5. As a primary project it has two weaknesses: crowding, and weak, trade-off-dominated metrics. A 2026 NTIRE-workshop paper already systematically sweeps StyleID's layer and timestep injection schedules (35 configs, 28k+ images), which pre-empts the most obvious "analysis" project. I rank it below Candidates A and B. If chosen, it must report Pareto curves (style score against content score) and a small human study, not single ArtFID numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **StyleID**: "Style Injection in Diffusion: A Training-free Approach for Adapting Large-scale Diffusion Models for Style Transfer", Chung et al. (author from memory), **CVPR 2024 Highlight**. It substitutes content K/V with style K/V in self-attention, with query preservation and attention temperature scaling — [CVF Open Access PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Chung_Style_Injection_in_Diffusion_A_Training-free_Approach_for_Adapting_Large-scale_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf); [project page](https://jiwoogit.github.io/StyleID_site/); [GitHub jiwoogit/StyleID](https://github.com/jiwoogit/StyleID) (489 stars) [Verified]
- **StyleAligned**: "Style Aligned Image Generation via Shared Attention" (Hertz et al., CVPR 2024; author and venue from memory). The official Google repo exists — [GitHub google/style-aligned](https://github.com/google/style-aligned) (1,316 stars; repo description does not state the venue) [Repo verified; venue unverified this session]
- **InstantStyle**: "InstantStyle: Free Lunch towards Style-Preserving in Text-to-Image Generation" — [GitHub instantX-research/InstantStyle](https://github.com/instantX-research/InstantStyle) [Verified repo; arXiv-only, no top venue found]
- **StyleSSP**: "StyleSSP: Sampling StartPoint Enhancement for Training-free Diffusion-based Method for Style Transfer", **CVPR 2025** per the official ByteDance repo — github.com/bytedance/StyleSSP (found via GitHub API search) [Verified repo listing]. STAM ("Zero-shot style transfer using diffusion model via attention modulation", CVPR 2025 pp. 6333–6343) appears only in a bibliography — [HAM arXiv 2603.24043](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24043) [Unverified]
- **Scheduled Style Injection** (Amey Sunil Kulkarni, arXiv 2605.26538, 26 May 2026, "Accepted to CVPR NTIRE 2026"). It schedules injection strength across decoder layers and timesteps and schedules ControlNet conditioning along the same axes. Decreasing schedules beat increasing ones, and cosine or sqrt schedules beat linear. Best config: ArtFID 27.04 versus StyleID 28.80, from 35 configurations and >28k images, with the ranking reported consistent across SD backbones — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26538); code [GitHub ameyskulkarni/scheduled_style_injection](https://github.com/ameyskulkarni/scheduled_style_injection) [Verified]
- Other 2025–2026 training-free style work: AttenST ([arXiv 2503.07307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07307)), HAM ([arXiv 2603.24043](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.24043)), StyleGallery ([arXiv 2603.10354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.10354)), and "Inversion-Free Style Transfer with Dual Rectified Flows" ([arXiv 2511.20986](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20986)) [Verified existence]
- **Metrics**: ArtFID = (1 + LPIPS)·(1 + FID). Its original paper reports strong agreement with a large human study (Spearman ≈ 0.93 across 13 NST models, 50k content-style pairs) — [ArtFID arXiv 2207.12280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12280v1) [Secondary numbers via search summary]. A 2023 ArtScore paper claims better human alignment than Gram loss and ArtFID (a single-paper claim) — [arXiv 2305.04923](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04923v1). A review notes that human evaluation is costly and hard to reproduce — [Evaluation in NST review, arXiv 2401.17109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.17109)
- **CSD** (style descriptor usable as a style-similarity metric): "Measuring Style Similarity in Diffusion Models", Somepalli et al., ECCV 2024 — [ECVA PDF](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/08294.pdf); [GitHub learn2phoenix/CSD](https://github.com/learn2phoenix/CSD) [Verified]

### Inferences
- **Evaluation weakness [Inferred]:** ArtFID's human-alignment evidence was gathered on pre-diffusion NST models by the metric's own authors. It is FID-based, so it needs many samples and is sensitive to the reference set. It also merges content (LPIPS) and style (FID) into one number, which hides the trade-off that every injection-strength knob moves along. Any "improvement" can be a different point on the same Pareto curve. This is the generative analogue of Candidate A's operating-point problem.
- **If chosen anyway [Hypothesis]:** "Training-free style-injection gains reported as single ArtFID numbers vanish when methods are compared at matched content preservation (LPIPS/DINO) on a common Pareto front." This mirrors Candidate A's protocol idea, is cheap on SD1.5 (about 3–4 GB fp16) and is course-feasible. Novelty is low given Scheduled Style Injection.
- **Rank [Inferred]:** third or fourth. It is safe and visual (good for the presentation), but not a recommended primary choice.

### Gaps
- I found no independent 2024–2026 audit of ArtFID/CSD human alignment on diffusion-based stylization. Absence of evidence, not evidence of reliability.

## Cross-cutting: feasibility on one 12 GB Titan V (Volta, sm_70)

### Takeaway
All three candidates fit on a Titan V with careful choices, but the software stack is the real risk, more than VRAM. Pin PyTorch to a CUDA 12.6 build (Volta has been dropped from PyTorch's CUDA 12.8+ and CUDA 13 wheels). bitsandbytes NF4 4-bit works on sm_70, but LLM.int8 does not. bf16-native VLMs (Qwen2.x-VL family) have documented fp16 attention overflow, and 7B VLMs do not fit in fp16.

### Cited Findings
- bitsandbytes official requirements: **NF4/FP4 quantization needs CC 6.0+** (works on Volta). **LLM.int8() needs CC 7.5+** (Turing or newer, so it does **not** work on Volta). 8-bit optimizers need 6.0+. PyPI Linux wheels target sm70 for CUDA 11.8–12.8 builds, but CUDA 13.0–13.2 builds drop sm70 (start at sm75) — [bitsandbytes installation docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/bitsandbytes/main/en/installation) [Verified]
- PyTorch: Volta was **dropped from CUDA 12.8/12.9 binaries in release 2.11** (to enable cuDNN 9.15.1), with Volta users directed to CUDA 12.6 builds — [PyTorch dev-discuss](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/dropping-volta-support-from-cuda-12-8-binaries-for-release-2-11/3290). PyTorch 2.12 keeps CUDA 12.6 as the legacy build for Maxwell, Pascal and Volta, since CUDA 13.x does not support them — [PyTorch dev-discuss 2.12](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/introducing-cuda-13-2-and-deprecating-cuda-12-8-release-2-12/3337). A notice says CUDA 12.6 wheels stop from **PyTorch 2.15**, which drops Volta entirely — [PyTorch dev-discuss notice](https://dev-discuss.pytorch.org/t/notice-cuda-12-6-wheels-will-no-longer-be-published-from-pytorch-2-15-drops-maxwell-pascal-volta/3432) [Verified via search snippets of official forum posts]
- Qwen2-VL fp16: transformers PR #33312 traced garbled fp16 output to attention weights overflowing to inf, then softmax producing NaN. The workaround zeroes infs in fp16. Qwen documentation advises against fp16 — [transformers PR #33312 mirror](https://github.hscsec.cn/huggingface/transformers/pull/33312); [HF discussion Qwen2-VL-7B #20](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct/discussions/20). The official vLLM recipe uses bf16 for Qwen2.5-VL — [vLLM Qwen2.5-VL recipe](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/recipes/en/stable/Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL.html) [Secondary via search summary]
- Qwen2.5-VL's official repo now redirects to QwenLM/Qwen3-VL (20k stars, last push 2026-01) — [GitHub QwenLM/Qwen2.5-VL → Qwen3-VL](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen2.5-VL) [Verified redirect]
- The LLaVA official repo (NeurIPS'23 Oral) exists, last push 2024-08 — [GitHub haotian-liu/LLaVA](https://github.com/haotian-liu/LLaVA) [Verified]
- Diffusers SD3: challenging under 24 GB even in fp16. Dropping T5 is documented — [diffusers SD3 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/api/pipelines/stable_diffusion/stable_diffusion_3) [Verified via search summary]

### Inferences
- **Recommended environment [Inferred]:** PyTorch ≤2.14 with cu126, bitsandbytes built for CUDA ≤12.8, and `attn_implementation="eager"` (needed anyway for attention-map hooks) or `"sdpa"`. FlashAttention-2 is unavailable on sm_70 (user-stated). Whether PyTorch's SDPA memory-efficient backend is used on sm_70 is likely but unverified. Never `pip install torch` unpinned: PyPI defaults to CUDA 13 wheels, which lack sm_70.
- **Model choices [Estimate]:**
  - LLaVA-1.5-7B: fp16 ≈ 14 GB, so it **does not fit**. NF4 ≈ 4–5 GB fits, with a quantization confound. 8-bit is impossible (LLM.int8 needs CC 7.5).
  - Qwen2.5-VL-3B: about 3.75B params, so fp16 weights ≈ 7.5 GB and fp32 ≈ 15 GB (does not fit). fp16 needs an overflow guard (clamp, or upcast softmax/attention to fp32). Cap max_pixels.
  - ~2B InternVL-class models: about 4–5 GB fp16; likely the same bf16→fp16 risk (inferred).
  - SD1.5: about 2–4 GB fp16 (trivial).
  - SD3-medium without T5: about 6 GB.
  - SDXL fp16: likely fits but has a known fp16 VAE issue (community knowledge, unverified here).
  - FLUX.1-dev (12B): does not fit in fp16.
- **Time budget [Estimate]:** about 3 weeks × ~20 usable GPU-h/day ≈ 400 GPU-h nominal. Realistically about 150–250 GPU-h after debugging. Candidate A needs about 40–80 GPU-h (comfortable), Candidate B about 60–150 GPU-h (tight at 1024²), and Candidate C about 20–40 GPU-h.

### Gaps
- I did not verify, on actual Titan V hardware, the fp16 stability of Qwen2.5-VL-3B or InternVL-2B, or the throughput of NF4 LLaVA. A 1-hour smoke test before 11-18 is strongly advised: load each model, run 50 POPE questions, check for NaN and the yes-ratio.
- Exact SD3.5-medium memory footprint: sources conflict (see Candidate B).

## Considered and rejected (in scope), plus directions excluded by user request

### Takeaway
Rejected in scope: (1) a new contrastive-decoding or attention-intervention hallucination method (saturated and undermined by the Mirage critique); (2) a new visual-token pruning method (about 30 CVPR 2026 papers); (3) style-injection method variants as the primary project (crowded, weak metrics, pre-empted by Scheduled Style Injection); (4) FLUX- or video-diffusion-based projects (do not fit 12 GB fp16 or the time budget). Excluded by user request: old-film/photo restoration, artist/style retrieval, minhwa/folk-art generation, and all 3D vision.

### Cited Findings
- New decoding or attention method for hallucination: 21 of 33 CVPR 2026 hallucination entries on an unofficial index are training-free interventions — [papernotes CVPR2026/hallucination](https://en.papernotes.org/CVPR2026/hallucination/). Contrastive-decoding POPE gains are reported as spurious — [arXiv 2504.10020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10020)
- New token pruning method: about 30 token-compression papers in one CVPR 2026 category — [papernotes CVPR2026/vlm_efficiency](https://en.papernotes.org/CVPR2026/vlm_efficiency/). Random baselines are often competitive — [ACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.802/)

- FlowEdit authors could not even run Stable Flow on their GPU (RTX A6000, 48 GB), a sign that some flow-editing baselines are memory-heavy — [FlowEdit arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08629v2)

**Excluded by user request (brief record only, not developed further)**

Inferred: real old films lack ground truth, so evaluation relies on no-reference metrics.
